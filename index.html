Chenglin Li 李承霖

First-year PhD Student · University of Michigan

Hi! I'm a first-year PhD student at the University of Michigan, specializing in Human-Computer Interaction and advised by Anhong Guo.

My research focuses on designing and evaluating multimodal systems that support lasting changes in behavior and workflows. I study how people work and build interactive systems that enhance human capabilities in real-world tasks.

I've been fortunate to collaborate with and learn from Yayuan Li, Xinyun Cao, Dhruv Jain, and Venkatesh Potluri. I've also served as a Graduate Student Instructor under Sugih Jamin.

Outside research, I enjoy badminton and performing Peking Opera.

Research interests: Human–AI interaction · Physical task guidance · Accessibility

Substantial, Decomposable, and Invisible: Visual Context Misalignment in Instructional Videos for Physical Tasks

Yayuan Li*, Chenglin Li*, Jingying Wang, Filippos Bellos, Anhong Guo†, Jason J. Corso†

Two studies with 56 participants examine how visual context misalignment in instructional videos affects physical task performance and user perception.

Abstract +

We compare in-context instructional videos (ICON) with conventional videos, then isolate four visual attributes: task object intrinsics, task object state, environmental context, and observational context. Aligned videos improve completion quality and speed, while individual misalignments degrade performance.

RAVEN: Realtime Accessibility in Virtual ENvironments for Blind and Low-Vision People

Xinyun Cao, Kexin Phyllis Ju, Chenglin Li, Venkatesh Potluri, Dhruv Jain

RAVEN enables blind and low-vision people to query and modify virtual 3D scenes in real time using natural language.

Abstract +

Evaluations with eight blind and low-vision participants and six Unity developers explore how conversational programming can support personalized accessibility, along with the challenges of reliability, transparency, and trust.
